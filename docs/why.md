# Why Open Wearable BCI Exists

*"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." (John 3:16, KJV)*

*Project lead: Rob Roy. Contact: see the [README](/README.md#contact).*

Friends,

I want to tell you plainly what this project is and why we're doing it in the open.

Technology is getting close to reading signals from the human brain without surgery: headbands, wristbands, and other devices you can put on and take off (*established* for simple signals like attention and deliberate muscle commands; *likely* to keep improving). Some devices can also send signals back into the brain. Used well, this could be a real mercy, helping a person who can't move or speak easily take part more fully in work and community (*likely*, within limits; most voice restoration today still needs implants). Used badly, it could become the worst kind of control: reading people without their knowledge, scoring them, selling what goes on in their heads, or nudging what they believe and want.

Both futures are on the table. We want to help build the first one and refuse the second, and we want to do it where anyone can check our work.

## Where we start

We start with God, not with the device. Every person is made in God's image (Genesis 1:27). A person is not a signal to optimize or a market to capture. Wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord (Proverbs 9:10), not with bandwidth. A faster connection won't make anyone wiser, kinder, or holier, and we won't pretend it will. Jesus came "not to be ministered unto, but to minister" (Mark 10:45, KJV). We follow that pattern, and any tool we touch must serve the same way.

## Why open source

Tools that touch the mind shouldn't be black boxes. When the code, designs, and reasoning are open, anyone can find our mistakes, test our claims, and hold us to our promises. That includes engineers, clinicians, pastors, disabled users of assistive tech, and skeptics. If we drift, we want to be caught.

## What we're actually doing

Our first stage only reads simple signals, such as "confirm," "pause," and "I'm overloaded." We are not stimulating anyone's brain. Right now this is research on paper. Nothing has been clinically tested, and nothing here is medical advice.

## What we will do

- **The person stays in charge.** A device acts only on what the wearer deliberately chooses. Every action gets confirmed, and anything that can't be undone takes two steps.
- **The mind stays private.** Raw brain data stays on the device, and only simple commands ever leave it. Nothing is sold or pooled, and nothing trains other systems without a separate, clear yes.
- **Taking it off is safe and free.** The wearer holds an off switch, there's no penalty for disconnecting, and by default there's a weekly day of rest from the device, on a day the wearer chooses.
- **A named person is accountable for every use.** "The algorithm did it" is never an acceptable answer.
- **We're honest about what these devices can and can't do**, including how often they get it wrong.
- **The vulnerable come first.** No children take part in this stage, and the sick, the poor, and employees get stronger protections, not weaker ones.

## What we will never do

- Require one of these devices for a job, a service, a school, or a place in the community.
- Read anyone covertly, including bystanders who never chose to wear one.
- Use brain signals to change what someone believes, desires, or worships, or to manufacture a "spiritual" experience.
- Score people's inner lives for hiring, credit, insurance, policing, or church standing.
- Use neural data for interrogation, lie detection, criminal evidence, border decisions, or watching workers and students.
- Target ads or feeds from brain signals.
- Allow military or police use that controls people.
- Present any machine as a guide, an oracle, or a savior.

## How we're moving

Slowly, on purpose. Before anyone puts on a device, we're fixing simpler things first: clearer decisions, less noise, and a real stop button for every system. In this first stage, the project team checks each step. Independent outside review is required before any later stage. Every step needs freely given consent and a way back. If at any point the technology starts pulling people away from God, from each other, or from their own conscience, we stop.

Sincere believers agree the body belongs to God (1 Corinthians 6:19-20) but differ on what that means for enhancement beyond medical need. We aren't trying to settle that by decree. Anyone who chooses never to use these tools keeps their full place, voice, and leadership with us.

We're not building a new Babel to make a name for ourselves (Genesis 11:4). We're trying to make sure that when this technology arrives, and it is arriving, there are people in the room asking whether it serves the person in front of them.

Paul wrote, "Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others" (Philippians 2:3-4, KJV). He grounded it in the humility of Christ (vv. 5-8). That's the posture we're asking of ourselves here: nobody is drafted in, nobody is pushed aside, and the person wearing the device matters more than the device or the people building it.

If that matters to you too, come help. Start with [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md). I'm the one responsible for this project, so if you have a question, a concern, or a correction, bring it to me.
